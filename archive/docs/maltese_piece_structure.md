# Maltese Gear Cube: piece types, orientations, and pure subgroups

Computed from the verified move tables (`puzzles/maltese_gear/tables.py`) using the 192-point model in `scripts/group_order.py` (96 sticker points + 24 gear slots × 4 orientation points). Every number below was computed in this session unless marked `[bg]`.

## 1. Piece types

| Type | Count | Block size | Orientation group | Full group on pieces (projection of G) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Corners | 7 movable (+1 pegged, fixed by every move) | 3 stickers | Z3 | S_7 (order 5040) |
| Edges | 36 | 2 stickers | Z2 | Index-2 subgroup of S_2 ≀ S_18, order 8.39×10²⁰ |
| Gears | 24 slots | 4 orientation points | Z4 | S_24 (order 24!) |

- **Corners.** Block system: 7 triples of sticker points. The corner reference order is fixed by a GF(2) solve: the flip vector `[0, 1, 0, 0, 1, 1, 0]` (applied to the `CORNERS` table order) makes every move a pure rotation. `check_all_rotations() == 0`.
- **Edges.** The minimal block system of the edge projection has 18 blocks of size 2. So the 36 edges come in 18 pairs that move together. The edge projection is an index-2 subgroup of the wreath product S_2 ≀ S_18.
- **Gears.** Each gear slot is a block of 4 orientation points. Every gear move is a pure rotation with respect to the point labels.

## 2. Pure subgroups (elements that fix every other piece)

Pure subgroup K_T = pointwise stabiliser of all points outside type T (computed with sympy).

| Type | |K_T| | Piece permutations on T | Orientation kernel | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Corners | 1,837,080 | A_7 (order 2520) | Z3^6, twists summing to 0 mod 3 (order 729) | 3-cycles of corners available purely |
| Edges | 1.10×10²⁶ | Even part of the edge projection, transitive on 36 edges, order 4.20×10²⁰ | 2^18 | Sampled: every pure element is even, and total flip parity is even |
| Gears | 2.18×10⁴⁰ | A_24 (order 24!/2) | order 2^46 (index 4 in Z4^24) | 3-cycles of gears available purely |

Check: 1,837,080 = 2520 × 729; 1.10×10²⁶ = 4.20×10²⁰ × 2^18; 2.18×10⁴⁰ = 3.10×10²³ × 2^46.

## 3. Parity coupling

Every move has sign (−1) on the corner, edge and gear permutations simultaneously: `U, U', L, L', F, F'` all give `(C, E, G) = (−1, −1, −1)`. So for every reachable state the three signs are equal. One quarter turn flips all three, so a bad global parity is fixed by a single move.

## 4. Per-move effect

Each move moves 4 corners (a 4-cycle), 26 edges, and 16 gear slots (cycles of 8, 4, 4).

## 5. Pure isolating macros found so far

- **Edges (pure).** A depth-4 commutator search over conjugated atomic moves finds pure edge macros: 12 edges moved, all 12 flipped, cost 28 quarter turns. Example (U/L/F quarter turns): `U U L U U U U L' U' U' L L L L U U L U' U' U' U' L' U' U' L' L' L' L'`. Pure edge macros with 12 edges and 4, 8 or 12 flips also appear at cost 28–32.
- **Corners and gears (pure).** None found by commutator search up to depth 4. These are covered by the pure subgroups above, which are guaranteed to contain them; explicit words need a word-tracking stabiliser chain (see `docs/piece_isolation_algorithms.md`, §9).

## 6. Consequences for the solver

- Corners and gears can each be sorted by a pure 3-cycle group (A_7 and A_24) and fixed for orientation (Z3 sum-zero, Z4 kernel).
- Edges cannot be sorted by 3-cycles of single edges: the edge group is imprimitive (pairs). Edge work is block 3-cycles (of pairs) plus flips and within-pair swaps, with the overall parity coupled to the other two types.
- A solver therefore needs: one parity move, then type-by-type pure work, with the cross-type orientation constraints (corner twist sum, gear rotation sum, edge flip parity) checked at the end.

Verification scripts: `scratchpad/explore3.py` … `explore12.py` (point model, block systems, pure subgroups, commutator search). The one-point lemma is checked by `scripts/verify_one_point_commutator.py`.

## 7. Targeted search for corner-only and gear-only macros (negative results)

Checked in this session:

- **Meet-in-the-middle** (words A, B with equal non-target effect, so A·B⁻¹ changes only the target type): no non-identity corner-only or gear-only element of total length ≤ 16. Radius 6, 7 and 8 per side, with hashed keys (`scratchpad/mitm3.py`, `mitm_gear.py`).
- **Commutator search** [X, Y] over conjugated atomic moves, with X from setups of depth ≤2 or ≤3 and Y from setups of depth ≤3 (about 6.6 million pairs, vectorised). Pure corner and pure gear commutators: none (`scratchpad/search2.py`).
- **Half-turns.** U⁴, L⁴ and F⁴ leave the corners unchanged. Together they generate only a group of order 8. Their normal closure has order 1.2×10⁶³, which is half of |N_C| = |G|/|corner projection| = 2.4×10⁶³. So corner-trivial elements are generated by conjugates of the half-turns, up to an index-2 coset.

Consequences:

- Any corner-only or gear-only macro that exists has length ≥ 17 quarter turns. The minimum is not known.
- The corner-only subgroup can only be reached by cancelling the edge and gear effects of a longer word with corner-trivial elements (normal closure of the half-turns). This is a search inside that group, not a short commutator.
- The word-tracking stabiliser chain gives words that exceed 3000 moves. It is not a usable solver as built.
